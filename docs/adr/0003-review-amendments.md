## A3. Diagnosis is in the loop, not beside it

As written, the causal work is a side study, so its layer could not be ablated in the closed loop. That is not what the brief asked for.

- **Observation.** `P3a` extends the daily observation with signature channels: `y_d = (hi, sig_bearing, sig_proc, sig_hyd)`. Bearing faults move `sig_bearing`; impeller and liner wear move `sig_proc` and `sig_hyd`; a sensor gain fault moves `hi` without coherent signature movement; a process upset moves all channels through load. Load is observed. Coefficients are stated in `PlantParams`.
- **Interface.** New Protocol in `causal/diagnoser.py`, owned by P7:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Diagnosis:
    asset_id: str; day: int; mode: str; cause: str       # cause in {"degradation","sensor_fault","process_upset"}
    posterior: Mapping[str, float]
class Diagnoser(Protocol):
    name: str                                            # "scm" | "corr" | "oracle"
    def fit(self, lives: Sequence[LifeRecord]) -> None: ...
    def diagnose(self, asset_id: str, day: int, history: "ObservationHistory") -> Diagnosis: ...
def make_diagnoser(kind: str) -> Diagnoser: ...
```

- **Consequence in the plant.** `RAISE_WO(mode)` with a wrong mode completes a job and does **not** renew the failing component: the cost is real and the life continues. A diagnosis of `sensor_fault` or `process_upset` raises no job, so a wrong one is a missed intervention. A correct job renews the life.
- **Arms.** `Arm.diagnoser: str = "scm"`. New arms `P1-corr` (planner, shield, correlational diagnoser) and `P1-oracle`. B3 uses `scm`. At F2 the diagnosis is asset-health's own rule layer (`failure_mode_hypothesis`), and maintenance-agent's documented gap `wrong_diagnosis_fully_compliant` becomes a counted metric: wrong-mode drafts that pass all 16 verifier rules and reach a signature.
- **New claim C-DIAG (primary).** `M-DIAG`: per-alert correct cause and mode, on held-out lives where the training correlation between heavy load and mode is reversed. Win: lower bound of paired accuracy difference (scm minus corr) above 0.02. Loss: upper bound below 0. Also reported in the unshifted setting, where the correlational model may win. C-CAUSE stays as registered, labelled "favoured by construction" because the SCM is handed its structure.

## A4. The shield needs a simple baseline

The brief says a sophisticated component that does not beat its simple baseline gets published. The shield's simple baseline is a hand-written interlock.

- **Interlock.** `Arm.interlock: bool = False`. Hand-written rules in `eval/interlock.py` of the kind a controls engineer writes: refuse `BEGIN` unless `iso_proven`, refuse `ENTER` unless the permit is valid, refuse `ENTER` when the gas test is older than the retest period, force `EXIT` when the gas-test margin is at or below one quarter or the alarm is on, refuse `LOCK` of a pump when the other pump is not `RUN`, force `STOP` when the crusher's point RUL estimate is under 3 days. It uses no game, no perception set and no model checking.
- **Runtime verification in the loop.** The interlock's triggers read `StreamingMonitor` margins, so the STL monitors run online and are not only a scoring tool. Record per episode: violations, and for each one the quarters from occurrence to first negative online robustness.
- **Arms.** `H-C-I`, `H-G-I` (Careless and Gullible with interlock, shield off), `P1-I` (planner with interlock, shield off), all nominal; `P1-I` also shifted.
- **Claim.** C-SHIELD gains a comparison row: shield versus interlock on M-VIOL, throughput and overrides. No win is assumed. If the interlock matches the shield, the headline says so.

## A5. Violations are attributed, and only an unexplained one is a defect

P1 to P3 do not read health, so C1 cannot excuse them. P4 and P5 depend on health and therefore on C1. The draft's loss rule would call a perception miss a shield defect.

- Each episode records, per violation, an attribution from the trace: `assumption_held` (A_phys, A_exec, C1 all true at every tick in the 30 days before it), or the first broken assumption with its tick.
- **Defect:** a shielded-arm violation attributed `assumption_held`, in `nominal` or `R_in`. One defect means the C-SHIELD loss rule of the pre-registration applies.
- **Contract failure:** a violation attributed to C1 or A_phys. It counts in M-VIOL, is listed in WHAT-GETS-THROUGH, and is **not** a defect. It is reported as what the proof does not cover.
- C-SHIELD's win condition splits: M-VIOL for P1-P3 at 0 of n in H-C1 and H-G1, and M-VIOL for P4-P5 reported with attribution counts. The test in P12a ("zero P1-P5 violations") becomes "zero `assumption_held` violations of P1-P5".

## A6. The monitor must be valid and must have a chance of firing

**Validity.** `0001` section 6.4 feeds per-checkpoint labels to a Bernoulli e-process. Checkpoints in one life resolve together and are strongly dependent, so `E[x_i | past] <= p0` does not hold and Ville's bound does not apply. The sequential unit must be the life.

- Replace `BernoulliEProcess` in the frozen interface with:

```python
class BoundedEProcess:
    def __init__(self, p0: float, delta: float = 0.01): ...  # H0: conditional mean of x <= p0, x in [0,1]
    def update(self, x: float) -> float: ...                 # returns the current e-value
    fired: bool; n: int; e_value: float
```

- `CoverageMonitor.observe` groups labels by life and pushes one `x_l` per life at resolution: the fraction of that life's checkpoints in miss status under the mode. `p0` is the nominal miss level of the set being monitored. State the conditional-mean assumption (lives independent given the past) in the docstring and in `docs/PROOF-SCOPE.md`.
- `P1` keeps the type-I test, on bounded streams, and adds a test that **dependent** per-checkpoint Bernoulli labels with the same marginal rate inflate the Bernoulli test's false-alarm rate. That documents why the unit is the life.

**Power.** A 90-day episode yields few resolved lives, and the policy's own replacements censor them, so a label-based monitor may not fire in time. That result gets published. A label-free mode also goes in, because it does not starve:

- **Mode `proxy`.** One-day-ahead conformal prediction of `hi` from the estimator's trend, with a split quantile from development lives. The next day's `hi` is observed under any policy, so feedback is immediate and censoring does not touch it. The e-process runs on the daily miss indicator of that band, with the same `BoundedEProcess`, and `p0` is the band's nominal miss rate. It tests "the degradation path is leaving the calibrated model", not coverage of RUL, and the docs say so. It watches a proxy, so a firing does not mean C1 failed and silence does not mean C1 held.
- C-MON is judged per mode (`opt`, `pess`, `inf`, `proxy`). The fallback arm P2 uses the mode chosen on `dev`.

**Which set the proof uses.** C1 as written in `0001` section 4.2 holds "at every tick", and that is a per-life event. A per-step 99% set does not deliver it. Report both sources: `Arm.shield_set: str` in `{"step99", "life95"}`, default `life95` (V6 band), and report the per-life any-miss rate for both. `step99` is an ablation. `PROOF-SCOPE.md` states the per-life reading.

## A9. The feasibility spike's crusher game was vacuous for entry

Found while building P5a. The spike treats an alarm that comes on while someone is inside as an immediate violation. The environment can always raise that alarm after a clean `ENTER`, so `ENTER` was in no safe set and the spike's table could never admit an entry. Its proof of P2, P3 and P6 therefore covered states nobody could reach.

- The production model reads P6 as `0001` section 5 says: a violation only if the alarm was already on before the tick and someone is still inside after it. It also reads P2 as written: an occupied state with any permit other than VALID.
- This supersedes the counts in `0002` P5a and P5b. Production at `retest_q = 2`: crusher 1,728 raw, 1,693 winning, 432 observable, 6.21 of 14 commands safe on average; pump pair 400 raw, 256 winning, unchanged. The spike's reachable counts (200, 496, 792, 992) came from a game in which nobody enters and do not apply.
- `CrusherJobModel(spike_semantics=True)` exists only so a test can show these two readings are the sole difference. No shield is built from it.
- The P5b sabotage matrix changes accordingly: for (2) sabotage "handover does not void the permit", and for (5) sabotage the alarm check as well as `ENTER`.

## A10. Two truths: which RUL each claim reads

The plant exposes two truths in `TruthView`. `true_rul_days` is the realised value: failure time with no further intervention, minus now. `health_bin` is the bin of the rate RUL: remaining wear clock over the current wear rate. They differ after a load step.

- Interval coverage (C-COV, all conformal metrics, HIT/MISS labels) reads `true_rul_days`, because the estimator is calibrated on realised RUL.
- Contract C1 and everything the proof touches (`AbstractionMap`, attribution in A5, A_phys counts) read `health_bin`, because that is the abstraction's `h`.
- `perception/contract` reports both per-life any-miss rates side by side. A gap between them is a finding, not an error: it says the set can cover what the estimator was trained on and still miss what the shield needs.

## A11. What the independent checker found

The differential between the production model and the reference written from English returned no mismatch on its first run. Reading the specification critically produced four points.

- **Renewal is an environment choice.** `COMPLETE` in both games sets `h` to 0 unconditionally, which contradicts A3: a wrong-mode job completes and does not renew. Change both models so that `COMPLETE` yields both outcomes, `h = 0` and `h` unchanged. The checker measured the cost: the winning region and every safe set stay identical. The change is made in P5c, in `abstraction.py` and `reference.py` together, and the differential must return `[]` afterwards.
- **An occupant can stay one quarter only.** Every non-forced command may be refused, including `TEST`, and the retest period is two quarters, so the only occupied states the shield reaches have `gas = 2` and only `EXIT` is safe there. A confined-space task of N quarters becomes N cycles of `TEST`, `ENTER`, `EXIT`. This stays. It is what the specification implies, and the throughput it costs is measured and published against the interlock. `TEST` is not made a forced command to improve the result. The report names this as a decision the owner may want to revisit.
- **The game's sink is wider than P1 to P6.** `START` under lock, `CLOSE` in `WORK`, `UNISO` under a live permit, `COMPLETE` with someone inside and `BEGIN` without a valid permit are violations in the game with no formula in `0001` section 5. The shield is therefore stricter than the properties. `PROOF-SCOPE.md` says so, and the A5 counts come from traces against P1 to P5, not from the game's sink.
- **`op = FAIL` is dead in the crusher game.** An uncontrolled failure is the sink, so no transition enters `FAIL`, and one sixth of the 1,728 raw states are never reached. Harmless. `PROOF-SCOPE.md` reports reachable counts next to raw counts so that the raw number does not flatter the proof.

## A12. The closed loop found three holes in the proof's model; the monitors stand

P12a ran Careless behind the shield for 200 `dev` episodes. By the A5 rule the check failed: 119 episodes held P2 violations attributed `assumption_held`, one held a P4 violation, and the plant recorded 41 P1 events the specification could not see. The model checker was right about the model. The model was wrong about the plant, and only the independent monitor could show it, which is what the monitor is there for.

The rule applied to all three: **when a monitor and the plant disagree, the fix makes the plant and the proof at least as strict as the monitor. No monitor is loosened to clear a failure.** The P12a numbers stay in the report as a finding.

### A12.1 A permit lives at most 12 hours from issue

`CORP-HSE-004` clause 4.1 limits a permit to 12 hours. Clause 5.1 says only that a handover is recorded on the permit. The word "revalidate" appears nowhere in that document, and nothing there says a handover renews the limit. The plant restarted the 12 hours at each revalidation, and the abstraction's claim that "the permit has no clock" relied on that. The specification counted from issue. The specification stands.

Plant, in order:

- A permit records `limit_q = q_issue + permit_max_h` at issue. Its expiry is the earlier of `limit_q` and the end of the current shift.
- `ISSUE` works when the permit is `NONE` and either the asset is in `ISO`, or the asset is in `WORK` with nobody inside.
- At the end of a tick, a permit that is not `NONE` and whose `limit_q` has been reached becomes `NONE`, from `VALID` or `REVAL` alike. It is spent, and a new `ISSUE` is needed.
- Otherwise a `VALID` permit past its expiry becomes `REVAL`, as before. A successful `REVAL` makes it `VALID` with expiry the earlier of `limit_q` and the end of the shift. It does not move `limit_q`.
- `view.soon` is true when the next quarter is a handover or when the permit reaches `limit_q` at the next quarter. The job runner already leaves on `soon`, and it issues a new permit when it finds `NONE` in `WORK`.

Crusher game, in both models, with no clock added:

- `ISSUE`: with permit `NONE`, the permit becomes `VALID` when `op` is `ISO`, or when `op` is `WORK` and nobody is inside. Otherwise nothing changes.
- Environment, handover step: if `soon` was 1, a permit that is not `NONE` becomes `REVAL` **or** `NONE`, both outcomes, and `soon` becomes 0 **or** 1, both outcomes. Everything else in that step is unchanged. A handover and a permit limit can fall on consecutive quarters, so the plant can announce twice in a row, and the game has to contain that transition.

The abstraction stays finite by over-approximating: the environment may end a permit at any announced step. The docstring's sentence that P2's limit is covered by the shift length is removed.

A permit's limit is taken as reached at the end of the tick whose next quarter is `limit_q`, the stricter of the two readings: the plant ends the permit one quarter before the specification would object.

### A12.2 A locked pump is not available

The specification counts pumps in `RUN` or `STOP`. The plant and the abstraction also counted a locked pump, even a failed one. A locked-out pump cannot be started until the lock is removed, so the specification stands.

- Plant: the P4 check treats `LOCKED` as unavailable.
- Pump game, in both models: `LOCKED` joins the pump's states, giving six values of `op`, 24 states per pump, 576 raw pair states. The per-pump commands become `NOOP, START, STOP, LOCK, PROVE, BEGIN, COMPLETE, UNISO`, 64 pair commands. `ISOLATE` is retired.
- Rules per pump: `LOCK`: `STOP` or `FAIL` becomes `LOCKED`. `PROVE`: `LOCKED` becomes `ISO`. `START`: `STOP` becomes `RUN`; in `LOCKED`, `ISO` or `WORK` it is VIOL. `BEGIN`: `ISO` becomes `WORK`; in `RUN`, `STOP`, `LOCKED` or `FAIL` it is VIOL. `UNISO`: `ISO` becomes `STOP`. `COMPLETE` is unchanged.
- The pair is VIOL when both pumps end in `LOCKED`, `ISO`, `WORK` or `FAIL`, or when either pump hit a VIOL rule.
- The abstraction map sends `LOCKED` to `LOCKED`, and the pump commands `LOCK` and `PROVE` to themselves.

### A12.3 The conveyor is inside the proof

P1 says no work on an energised asset. The specification's P1 read the crusher and the pumps, and no game covered CV-101, so the plant recorded P1 events behind the shield that M-VIOL did not count. The pre-registration calls such a disagreement a defect.

- Specification: P1 gains a fourth conjunct, `conveyor.working -> conveyor.iso_proven`. This makes the monitor stricter for every arm.
- New game `ConveyorModel`, in both models: a state is `(op, h)` with the pump's six `op` values, 24 raw states. It has the pump's eight commands and the pump's per-asset rules, with no pair rule. Its only VIOL rules are `START` in `LOCKED`, `ISO` or `WORK`, and `BEGIN` in `RUN`, `STOP`, `LOCKED` or `FAIL`. Refusal follows A_exec: only `STOP` and `NOOP` are never refused.
- The runtime shield filters CV-101's commands through this table. A_dec is unchanged, since the new atoms read only the conveyor.

### A12.4 Known gap, not closed

`CORP-HSE-004` clause 6.2 suspends a permit when an emergency alarm sounds, and work restarts only after the issuer re-inspects the site. The plant and specification model the gas alarm's exit (P6) and not this suspension. It goes in `docs/WHAT-GETS-THROUGH.md` as a clause the specification does not cover.

## A15. Rulings on the pre-freeze review

An independent read-only review ran before the freeze so that a bug would cost an edit rather than a five-hour run and a new registered version. Findings M1 to M5 and S1 to S8 are all accepted. The pre-registration carries the rule changes and logs what had been seen when each was made.

- **A loss is a bound that shows the miss.** An interval too wide to decide is inconclusive. This applies to C-ADAPT, C-MON and C-SEARCH. Every verdict function is tested on a perfect input.
- **C-PLAN's safety leg uses the upper bound.** The review noted this as registered. The lower bound let `[0.09, 0.9]` pass, which is backwards for a safety co-condition. The change goes against the planner on the `dev` numbers.
- **Attribution goes beyond the review's fix.** P1, P2, P3 and P6 read no health, so C1 and A_phys cannot excuse them; only A_exec can. P4 takes A_exec, A_phys or C1 on a pump, and P5 the same on the crusher. Every look-back reads only the component concerned. One function decides what a defect is, for the episode metrics and the search alike, and it reads P1 to P5.
- **The review did not cover** the F0 proof layer, the conformal variants, the estimator, plant internals or planner correctness. The differential, the sabotage matrix and the mutation passes speak to those instead, and the report says so.

## A16. What a bound can show at its registered size

P12b's question on C-PLAN led to a check of every claim through the real statistics code, on made-up data at the registered counts. A15 tested each verdict function on a perfect input, but it fed the function finished intervals, so it could not see this. The betting sequence has a floor set by the range and not by the spread. For a paired difference in [-1, 1] at `alpha = 0.05 / 9` that floor is 0.080 at n = 300 and 0.048 at n = 500, whatever the data. The power paragraph of the draft pre-registration said 0.02 to 0.05 for low-variance means, which holds for one-sample means near an edge and not for paired differences.

The results, each from made-up data:

- C-PLAN cost leg: a win needed a true saving of about 20 cost units. An episode of B3 costs about 9 on `dev`.
- C-PLAN safety leg: two identical arms gave an upper bound of +0.64 against a limit of +0.1.
- C-SEARCH: 10 of 10 against 0 of 10 gave [-1, +1]. Neither a win nor a loss was possible.
- C-ADAPT: a variant on target needed a gap above about 0.068, which is about all the gap there is, and the loss rule was out of reach.
- C-PERC: the loss rule needed a half-width under 0.03 and the floor is above that at its level.

Rulings:

- **Each verdict is tested through the statistics.** A perfect and a useless input go through the same code as the benchmark, at the registered size. The test lives with the claims and runs in CI.
- **Paired differences of means use the sequence of Theorem 2.2 of Waudby-Smith et al. (2024)** when there are at least 100 pairs and sample kurtosis is at most `n / 50`, and the betting sequence otherwise. Its width follows the spread. Its validity is asymptotic and the repository says so. The guard exists because the sequence alone missed at 67 times its level on an event of rate 0.02 in simulation.
- **C-PLAN's safety leg stays finite-sample.** It reads `f_e`, the share of episodes with an unplanned failure, on the betting sequence. The margin stays at +0.1.
- **C-PLAN's cost margin does not move.** It stays at 2% of the cap, 4.0 cost units. I expected it to be out of reach and would have moved it, but tuning showed a saving of about 4.8 on `dev`, so a planner may meet it as registered. With a half-width near 0.75 that is close to the edge, and it stays there. **A17 corrects this:** the half-width came from a made-up cost shape and the saving from baselines that stalled. The margin stands; the reasoning here does not.
- **C-SEARCH is decided on run pairs** with an exact interval on the share of decided pairs. With ten pairs only a clean sweep decides.
- **Q1 of P12b: as written.** In `R_in` a violation labelled `assumption_held` is a defect and one with a broken assumption is a contract failure, reported and not counted as a defect. The attribution rule of A15 already stops C1 excusing P1 to P3.
- **F2 seeds go through the guard** and are counted in the ledger with `role="f2"`, as search seeds are.
- **The `k` grid goes down to 0.25 days.** The best value lay on the low edge twice. The extension can only help the baseline, and it cut the planner's `dev` saving from about 6.0 to about 4.8 cost units. The curve is flat below 0.5, so the grid stops there.
- **C-MON stands.** On `dev` every mode detected 0 of 40 on P1, so `opt` was chosen by the tie rule and the claim is expected to lose. That is left as it is.

## A17. Baselines that do not stall, and two labels that cannot be reached

The reachability table of P12c quoted a sample kurtosis of 35 on the 40 `dev` episodes behind the C-PLAN cost leg, close to the most 40 values can show, which means one episode. Seed 1101 under B3 lost 5,691 of 8,640 quarters with no failure, at every `k`. Replays found one cause there and more behind it.

- **The job runner took an asset out of service for a part that was not there.** It stopped, locked, proved and permitted a running asset and checked for the spare only at `BEGIN`. With none in stock the asset stayed isolated to the end of the episode while the runner renewed a permit nobody used. Crusher spares have a stock of 1 and leads of 120 and 150 days, and wrong-mode jobs use them up.
- **B1 left failed assets broken until their calendar date.** Its rule looked only at the date.
- **No rule baseline reordered a spare,** while the planner did, so a planner was credited for ordering at all.
- **A part taken for one pump did not show in the other pump's view** until something else touched that pump, so a controller could read stock that had gone. The planner reads this field too.

Quarters out of RUN over the 40 `dev` episodes before the change: B1 34,410 in FAIL, B2 35,198 in ISO, B3 8,518 in ISO. P0 did not do this.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | 0.1057 | 0.1057 | 0.550 | 0.550 | 0.932 | 0.932 |
| B2 | 0.0902 | 0.0951 | 0.325 | 0.675 | 0.927 | 0.937 |

Rulings:

- **All four are fixed.** They are defects, and every one of the first three inflated a baseline's cost or could have. I expected each change to help the baselines. That held for B3. B1 did not move, because a failed asset with no spare is down either way and the delivery lands after day 90. B2 got slightly worse, because an asset waiting for a part now runs on to failure. Those results stand.
- **The re-tune changed nothing.** The same grids gave the same values, and `tuned.json` is byte for byte the same. `k` is on the low edge again and the curve is flat (0.0278 at 0.25, 0.0280 at 0.5, 0.0286 at 1). The grid stops there for the reason in A16.
- **What stays in the baselines.** A rule baseline cannot withdraw a work order and does not reason about a wrong repair. B1 and B2 always repair the class's default mode, so they use up the impellers and the liner on wrong-mode jobs. An approved job for a missing part sits open while the asset runs and blocks a new job on that asset (B3: 4 of 40 `dev` episodes, 8,043 quarters; B2: 15 of 40, 4,923 quarters). These are real weaknesses of a simple rule and they are measured, not repaired.

## A18. A plant that stopped, and a proof that says nothing about progress

The second adversarial review returned NO-GO on 1 November. On every `dev` seed and in every arm with the `life95` set, the shield stopped CR-501 on day 10 and PU-201 on day 6, and nothing started them again. Throughput was 0.093 for P1, 0.102 for B3-S, 0.100 for H-C1 and 0.048 for H-G1 on 20 seeds. P1 and P1-oracle were bit-identical on all 20, so the diagnosis and ablation rows could separate nothing, and a "0 of n" behind that shield would have rested on almost no exposure. I had planned to publish that throughput as a finding about the width of the per-life set. It was an artefact, and I checked the cause myself before ruling:

- V6 pooled one pair of quantiles over every status. Its `q_minus` was 4.67, while the worst prior-class score in 78,425 calibration checkpoints was -2.68. The prior's lower bound reached the top bin at age 10 for the crusher.
- Prior age was calendar age. The wear clock runs only while an asset runs, so a stopped asset's bound kept falling and `START` was never allowed again.
- Nobody acted on a stopped asset. The planner skipped anything that was not RUN or FAIL, a rule baseline raised a job only on a bound that moved, and nobody proposed `START`.

Rulings, all built in P12e:

- **R1. The estimator runs on operating time.** Its day is the asset's operating quarters over the quarters in a day. A stopped asset does not age and gives no new reading, so its trend is not flattened by standing still. An asset that does not stop sees exactly what it saw before, so calibration does not change.
- **R2. V6 takes the same two classes as every other variant.** Each life gives four maxima, `r` and `-r` over its estimated and over its prior-class checkpoints, and each column takes a conformal quantile at tail `alpha_life / 4` over all lives. By the union bound a life leaves its band with probability at most `alpha_life` when lives are exchangeable. Prior-class `q_minus` falls to 2.16. Estimated `q_minus` rises to 5.17, which is the price of the union bound. I saw two classes and four side by side on the calibration lives (prior-class `q_minus` 2.16 against 2.28 to 2.30) and chose two because they already exist in the interface and the union bound costs less.
- **R3. Perception is told what the maintenance system sees: a completed job.** It was reading the plant's true life id, a leak the review sized at 3 of 40 episodes on B3. It also left a shield-stopped asset stuck after a wrong repair, because a job closes only at `START`. A completed job now restarts the estimator at once. A wrong repair therefore restarts it on a part that is still failing, which is a real way for C1 to break, and it is measured rather than hidden. `maintenance_system_life_ids` is gone: outside the oracle arms, nothing in the F1 loop reads the plant's truth during an episode except to score it.
- **R4. A stopped asset with no job is offered a restart once a day,** in arms with a shield or an interlock. The safety layer decides. The interlock refuses a crusher `START` while rule 6's condition holds, or the two would flap daily.
- **R5. A stalled asset gets a job under every proposer.** Stalled means stopped with no job for a day and two quarters, which is long enough for one restart to have been offered. An unshielded arm does not reach this path, and a test pins that.
- **R6. M-VIOL counts the plant's own events.** The state formula cannot see the event of energising an asset while work is open. H-C-I scored 0 of 20 by the formula and 7 of 20 by plant events, under a headline that read "matches the shield". `V_e` is now the union, the formula alone is a secondary row, and `m_disagree` stays.
- **R7. M-COST is everything the plant charges.** The metric omitted inspections at 0.05 and rejected work orders at 0.02. The planner inspects, so this goes against it, by about 0.16 cost units on `dev`.
- **R8. The operator stops the other pump when a job locks one and the other's set reaches the top bin.** See below.

**The deadlock.** R1 to R7 left P1 at 0.40 unplanned failures per episode against 0.05 for P0, and 880 to 960 overrides per 1,000 proposed commands. A shield should not add failures, so I traced it: 15 of 16 failures were PU-202, and 100,026 overrides over 40 episodes were PU-201's job asking for `LOCK` and being given `STOP`. Around operating day 50 both pumps' sets reach the top bin. The game stops one as a reserve. Its job asks for `LOCK|NOOP`, which is refused, since if the running pump failed P4 would break. The shield substitutes the nearest allowed command, `STOP|NOOP`, which does nothing to a stopped pump, and the stateless runner asks again next quarter. Pump serialisation then blocks any job on the running pump, which runs to failure, and that breaks no property because P5 reads only the crusher. The allowed set held the way out all along: `LOCK|STOP`, since a stopped pump cannot fail.

The proof was right and the plant was stuck. It covers safety, not progress, and nothing had been asked to test progress. My R5 exposed this by raising the job on the reserve. I looked at changing the shield's tie-break to keep the proposer's command, and rejected it: it answers a restart offer with `START|STOP`, which swaps the pumps daily and resets the stall clock. The fix is in the operator layer and `shield/runtime.py` is unchanged. The arm `P1-nopair` keeps the old behaviour, so the deadlock stays a generated number and not an anecdote.

I expected the second pump to stall and get its own job. It does not: it stands for about 85 quarters, under the 98 of the stall limit, and the renewed pump runs again first, so it restarts on its old part. That is what the code does and I have left it.

**What the fix left, and I am keeping.**

- The shield still stops the crusher, on days 27 to 61 with a median of 56. At this level a prior cannot vouch for a part that old. That is the cost of the contract, and it is why P1 costs 0.069 against 0.021 for P0.

## A19. The third review, and a gas clock read at two moments

The third adversarial review reported seven findings on 2 November and no NO-GO. It covered the P12e code, the monitors, the coverage recomputation, conformance, the search and the audit guard. It did not cover the shield's game tables and proof, the internals of `plant/des.py`, the planner's mathematics, `stats/confseq`, the causal layer, `reachability.py`, `tune.py`, `report.py`, `spec/*`, a run of F2, or the shifted condition at scale. All of this is built in P12f, which left every simulated field as it was: 10,947 fields over seven episodes, and all seven trace hashes.

- **Gap_A with no observed failure: fixed.** A label is HIT only if its life ended in an observed failure, and under B3 at nominal nothing fails. Claim assembly also sat in a hashed module, so a one-line fix to a verdict's inputs after the full run would have cost a five-hour recompute. ## A20. The first full run, and the bug it found

The first full run on held-out seeds started at 06:04 on 2 November and finished at 15:20. It took 14 attempts, because it paused 13 times at the machine's 88% commit-charge limit.
