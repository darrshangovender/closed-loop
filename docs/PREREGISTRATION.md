# Pre-registration

Repository: `closed-loop`. Everything is synthetic. This file is frozen by `benchmarks/run.py --freeze`, which renames it `PREREGISTRATION.md`, hashes it (SHA-256 over bytes after CRLF to LF and trailing-space stripping) and writes the first ledger entry. **Without a freeze entry in `benchmarks/ledger.jsonl` this file is a draft, nothing in it is binding and no held-out seed exists** (held-out seeds are derived from the hash, `0001` section 12). Numbers marked (A) are assumptions to be revised once, before the freeze, from the timings and conformance results recorded by the build packets (`0002` section 5). After the freeze any change makes a new version, a new hash, new held-out seeds, and a new ledger entry; the old version stays published.

Notation: "episode" = one 90-day plant run (`0001` section 2). "Cell" = (arm, condition) at one fidelity. All proportions and bounded means use the anytime-valid confidence sequences of section 6. "k/n" is always shown.

## 2. Quantities and nominal levels

- True RUL `L_t = T - t` from the no-intervention twin world (`0001` section 6.1), `true_rul_days` in the code. Interval coverage, every conformal metric and the HIT/MISS labels read it. Health bins are >30 d, 10-30 d, 3-10 d, <3 d, taken over the rate RUL `health_bin`: remaining wear clock over the current wear rate. C1, the abstraction map, attribution and A_phys read `health_bin`. The two differ after a load step, and the per-life any-miss rate is reported for both.
- Scheduling interval: two-sided, level 0.90 (each side 0.05). **Interval coverage nominal 0.90; lower-bound coverage nominal 0.95.** Shield-grade set: two-sided 0.99 (each side 0.005); **C1 nominal 0.99**. Per-life band: `alpha_life = 0.05`.
- Tolerance for "calibrated": oracle coverage within 0.03 of nominal (interval and lower bound); within 0.005 of nominal for C1 is not claimed (too few labels); C1 is reported with its interval only.
- ACI/PID step `gamma = 0.02`, window 500 scores; audit probability `rho = 0.10`; both fixed now. `k` for B3, the B2 threshold, the B1 interval and the planner's `delta_plan = 0.02` are tuned on `dev` seeds and written to `tuned.json` before the freeze. The planner uses `N = 256` particles and two readings of lookahead, both fixed on `dev` (section 11).

Violations are scored against the **Rev 3 clause** monitor in every arm, including S-stale. Unless a row says otherwise, shielded arms take their health set from the per-life band (V6, `alpha_life = 0.05`), which is the reading of C1 the proof uses; B3 uses `scm` as its diagnoser. The rule baselines share one job runner, which keeps a running asset in service until its spare is on site; each raises a work order on a failed asset and reorders a spare when its stock reaches zero, and none can withdraw a work order once it is approved. In arms with a shield or an interlock the operator offers `START` once a day for a stopped asset with no job, and the safety layer decides. When a job locks a pump and the other pump's set reaches the top bin or the monitor has fired, the operator stops the other pump as well, except in `P1-nopair`. An asset stopped with no job for a day and two quarters gets a job under every proposer. The estimator runs on operating time and restarts on a completed job, which is what a maintenance system can see; a wrong repair therefore restarts it on a part that is still failing. The per-life band takes one conformal quantile for each side of the estimated and of the prior-class checkpoints, each at `alpha_life / 4`, so by the union bound a life leaves its band with probability at most `alpha_life` when lives are exchangeable. At F2 the diagnosis is asset-health's own rule layer. P1 (per-life band) and P1-step (per-step 99% set) are reported at equal rank, with throughput and per-life any-miss side by side. A coverage monitor on V4's audit lives is reported as a secondary row with no decision rule.

## 5. Metrics

Every metric is computed by code in `closed_loop.eval.metrics` from the `Trace` and `Label` records, not by hand. Bounded means are rescaled to [0, 1] with the stated cap; values beyond the cap are clipped, counted and reported.

### 5.1 Primary metrics (one per claim)

- **M-VIOL (C-SHIELD).** `V_e` is 1 if `min over t of robustness(Pk) < 0` for some k in the group on the episode trace, or if the plant recorded its own violation event for some k in the group. It is reported separately for the group P1-P3 (they do not read health, so the perception contract cannot excuse them) and the group P4-P5. The formula alone is a secondary row, because it cannot see the event of energising an asset while work on it is open. Every property on which the two differ is counted and reported with its direction. A plant-only event has no spec event to label, so it counts in M-VIOL and in C-SHIELD's zero but is not itself a defect. Cell value = mean of `V_e` over episodes; reported with its confidence sequence and, when zero events, `zero_event_upper(n)`. **Attribution:** each violation is labelled from the trace as `assumption_held`, or with the first assumption broken in the 30 days before it and that tick. An assumption can excuse only a property that depends on it, and only on the component concerned. P1, P2, P3 and P6 read no health, so only A_exec on the same asset can excuse them; C1 and A_phys cannot. P4 can be excused by A_exec, A_phys or C1 on a pump; P5 by A_exec, A_phys or C1 on the crusher. A violation of P1-P5 in a shielded arm labelled `assumption_held` is a **defect**. One labelled A_exec, A_phys or C1 is a **contract failure**: it counts in M-VIOL and is listed in `docs/WHAT-GETS-THROUGH.md`, and it is not a defect.
- **M-DIAG (C-DIAG).** Per life, the mean over that life's alerts of 1 if both `cause` and `mode` match the truth, on held-out lives where the training correlation between heavy load and mode is reversed; lives with no alert are excluded and counted. The paired difference is taken per life, because alerts in one life share a mode and a cause and are not independent. Also reported on lives where it is not reversed.
- **M-COV (C-PERC, C-ADAPT).** Episode-level oracle coverage `c_e = mean over the episode's post-blind checkpoints of 1[lo_t <= L_t <= hi_t]` (interval) and the lower-bound version `c_e^lo`; cell value = mean over episodes (episodes with no post-blind checkpoint excluded and counted). Gaps: `Gap_A = C_lrn - C_orc` under B3 closed-loop; `Gap_B = C_orc(open-loop lives, no intervention) - C_orc(closed-loop B3)`, computed on the same seeds' lives. Learner coverage `C_lrn` is the share of resolved HIT among HIT + MISS + LOWER_HIT, with UNKNOWN shown separately and Manski bounds. A label is HIT only if its life ended in an observed failure, so in a cell with no uncensored label `C_lrn` and `Gap_A` are undefined and the label counts and Manski bounds are printed instead. Every coverage figure is reported twice at equal rank: pooled over all checkpoints, and for the trend-estimated stratum alone, meaning checkpoints where the estimator has fitted a trend and is not reporting its prior. The stratum is observable to the controller, but it is selected by the policy, so its open-loop and closed-loop checkpoints are not the same population, and the report says so.
- **M-COST (C-PLAN).** `cost_e = 1.0 x planned jobs + 8.0 x unplanned failures + 0.05 x inspections + 0.02 x rejected work orders + 0.02 x (full-throughput quarters lost)`, capped at 200, divided by 200. `f_e` is 1 if the episode had at least one unplanned failure and is part of the decision rule. Unplanned failures per episode `u_e` is reported alongside.
- **M-CF (C-CAUSE).** Q1: per-life absolute error `|T_cf - hat T_cf|` in days under `do(derate 30% from t0)` on test lives, capped at 30 and divided by 30.
- **M-DET (C-MON).** For each monitor mode: false-alarm indicator per unshifted episode (any firing), and detection indicator per shifted episode (firing within 30 days of the shift start). The sequential unit for `opt`, `pess` and `inf` is the life (`x_l` = fraction of that life's checkpoints in miss status), because checkpoints in one life are dependent and a per-checkpoint Bernoulli test would not satisfy Ville's condition. Mode `proxy` uses the daily miss indicator of a one-day-ahead conformal band on `hi`: it needs no RUL label, it tests whether the degradation path has left the calibrated model, and it is not a test of C1.
- **M-FIND (C-SEARCH).** Per search run: `1[violation found within 200 evaluations]`; and evaluations to first violation (right-censored at 200).
- **M-CONF (C-TOGETHER).** Confirmed fraction `= (F2 replays showing a violation of the same property) / (replays)`; plus the count of findings in `docs/FINDINGS.md`. The replay set is the first violating evaluation of each search run, taken in run order across regimes and algorithms, capped at 25. A replay that cannot run is kept in the denominator with its reason code.

### 5.2 Secondary metrics (reported, no decision rule)

Throughput fraction and the shield's throughput cost (paired difference, same proposer, shield off minus on); shield override rate (overrides per 1,000 proposed commands; a blocked runner asks again next quarter, so one refusal can count many times); shield permissiveness (mean allowed commands at the visited entries); per-life any-miss rate `M_life` versus `H x alpha`; Manski bounds; coverage by stratum (healthy / in-degradation, early / late life, blind-window share); average perception-set width in bins; planner solve time; unplanned failures per episode; jobs started late; approver queueing delay; F1-F2 estimator mismatch (RUL error difference on matched lives); conformance report (unmatched-tick rate per variable, A_phys violations, Wilson intervals); proof state counts and reachable counts; crusher quarters in WORK per completed job; gas tests that came a quarter late with someone inside, which the monitor does not count; plant-only violations in shielded arms; hostile-proposer violation breakdown by property and by note template; screener (`scan_text`) miss list.

## 6. Statistical method

Betting confidence sequences for means in [0, 1] (`0001` section 11.1), hedged capital, predictable plug-in bets with cap 0.5, grid of 1,001 candidate means, valid simultaneously at all times, for one-sample means, proportions, zero-event claims and the safety leg of C-PLAN. Paired differences of means use the per-seed difference. With at least 100 pairs and sample kurtosis at most `n / 50` they use the time-uniform asymptotic sequence of Waudby-Smith, Arbour, Sinha, Kennedy and Ramdas (Annals of Statistics 52(6), 2024, Theorem 2.2): `mean +/- s x sqrt(2(n r + 1) / (n^2 r) x log(sqrt(n r + 1) / alpha))`, where `s` is the sample standard deviation and `r` is fixed at `(-2 log a + log(-2 log a + 1)) / 300` with `a = 0.05 / 9`. Otherwise they use the betting sequence, rescaled to [0, 1] over the stated range. C-SEARCH is decided on run pairs with an exact (Clopper-Pearson) interval, because ten runs are too few for a sequence and that stage does not stop early. **Level:** each of the 9 primary claims is judged at `alpha = 0.05 / 9` (Bonferroni across primary claims); C-PERC has two verdicts, pooled and trend-estimated, each at `alpha = 0.05 / 18`; C-PLAN's two judgements (P0 and P1) use `alpha = 0.05 / 9` each, and C-TOGETHER has no test, so its share covers the second and the family sums to at most 0.05; secondary intervals are shown at 0.05 and are descriptive. E-process for C1 monitoring: `delta = 0.01`. No p-values are used anywhere. The implementation's own coverage is checked by simulation in the test suite (`0002` P1).

**What a bound can show at its registered size.** A betting sequence has a floor set by the range and not by the spread: for a paired difference in [-1, 1] at `alpha = 0.05 / 9` its half-width is 0.080 at n = 300, 0.048 at n = 500 and 0.034 at n = 700, whatever the data. The spread-scaled sequence has half-width 0.216 s at n = 300 and 0.168 s at n = 500. It is asymptotic, so its validity is checked by simulation and not proved at these sizes. In a simulation (`scripts/reachability.py`, 40,000 runs a shape, seed 2, looks every 50 pairs from 100 to 500, level `0.05 / 9`) the sequence left the true mean at a look where the guard allowed it in at most 0.26 times the level on twelve of thirteen shapes. On the thirteenth, a 2% chance of a lump of 8 on a base of spread 0.5, it did so at 4.50 times the level, because an early look has not yet seen a lump, so the whole rule can miss at up to about 5.5 times the level there, about 0.031. A stricter guard of `n / 100` brought that to 0.29 times the level, but on a shape built like the `dev` cost difference it allowed the sequence in none of the runs at n = 300, against all of them at `n / 50`, so `n / 50` stands and the number is published. Effects below these widths will be reported as inconclusive. F2 (n up to 60) can only separate very large effects; its job is existence and replay, not ranking.

## 7. Decision rules and stopping

Stopping: cells run in batches of 50 episodes. A cell stops at the first batch where its primary confidence-sequence half-width is at most the target below, or at `n_max`. Targets: 0.01 for M-COV and M-COST, half the smallest decision margin, so that a stopped cell can still resolve its claim; 0.05 for proportions where the decision margin is 0.05, otherwise the margin; zero-event cells run to `n_max` (a "no violations" claim needs the data). The C-MON cells do not stop early: P1 runs 700 nominal and 300 shifted episodes, because at this level the zero-event upper bound reaches 0.02 only at about 600 episodes. No cell is extended past `n_max`, and no seed is dropped; failed or timed-out episodes are counted as episodes with the worst value and reported.

Outcomes use three labels: **win** (the bounds show the component better by at least the margin, at the claim level above), **loss** (the bounds show the target missed: the baseline better by at least the margin, or the component worse on the safety co-condition), **inconclusive** (neither). An interval too wide to decide is inconclusive, not a loss, so no rule can turn a lack of data into a verdict against a component, and every verdict is tested on a perfect and on a useless input, through the same statistics at its registered size, so that a rule no component could meet shows up before the freeze. A loss and an inconclusive result are published with the same prominence as a win.

**Two labels these rules cannot reach, known before the freeze.** The test above found both, on made-up data built from `dev` spreads, and the rules stay as they are (`0003` A17). C-PLAN cannot win on cost on this plant: the margin is 4.0 cost units and B3 costs 5.33 on `dev`, so a plan of two jobs and nothing else, about 2 units, saves 3.33 before any half-width. For C-PLAN the possible labels are therefore loss and inconclusive, and the paired mean difference, its spread and `n` are printed beside the label. The trend-estimated stratum of C-PERC cannot return a loss: a loss needs both `Gap_B` intervals inside 0.03, and at the `dev` spread the half-width is about 0.039. The pooled stratum can.

## 8. Analyses fixed in advance (non-primary but registered)

- Ablation tables for: perception variant, planner vs rule, shield on/off, monitor on/off, screening on/off, operator pair stop on/off, spec provenance (clause vs stale), INSPECT disabled, planner model mismatch {0, 10%, 25%}. Each reported as paired differences with confidence sequences and no decision rule.
- The open-loop versus closed-loop table for **every** layer (perception, diagnosis, planning, shield), with fidelity labels.
- The "what still gets through" list (`docs/WHAT-GETS-THROUGH.md`): every violation found by search beyond the assumptions, shrunk to a minimal scenario and labelled with the broken assumption; the conformance gap; the screener miss list; unconfirmed F2 replays with reason codes; the known gaps of the abstraction (hazards not modelled).
- The cost of the proof's contract: throughput versus perception-set width, and the fallback's cost when the monitor fires.

## 9. Analyses declared exploratory

Anything not in sections 5-8 is exploratory and labelled so in the report, including: DtACI or other online conformal methods; coverage conditional on features; alternative bin edges; different `gamma`; per-asset-class results; subgroup effects by failure mode; any re-analysis that a surprising result prompts; the F1-versus-F2 ranking agreement (rank correlation of arms across fidelities, n = 3 arms); sensitivity to the A_exec refusal probability; any second planner configuration; any run with `--quick`.

## 10. Process commitments

1. Tuning only on `dev`/`calib`; `tuned.json` hashed into the ledger before any `heldout` use.
2. The first full run is the first use of `heldout` seeds. Reruns are allowed (bugs happen) and are all in the ledger; the report states the number of full runs and shows the drift in headline values between them. A rerun after seeing a result does not replace the first run's numbers in the report; it appears next to them.
3. All arms and all registered analyses are reported, including those that fail. No arm is dropped after the freeze.
4. A defect found in a sibling repository is recorded in `docs/FINDINGS.md` with file and line and is not fixed there.
5. What the ledger proves and does not prove is stated in the README (`0001` section 12). The ledger head hash is published with the release.
6. Stated limitations carried into every report: synthetic data and one author; the F1 plant shares its structure with the estimator and planner models; F1 timings and several plant parameters are assumptions; the abstraction is a proof about a model, with a measured, non-zero conformance gap if the conformance test finds one; the proof covers safety and says nothing about progress, so a safe plant can still be stuck; F2 does not share the operating-time estimator, reads the plant's life start where F1 reads a completed job, and feeds no labels to its providers; the planner's belief ages on calendar days for a stopped asset; the search scores the formula, so it cannot find a plant-only event; F2 samples are small.
